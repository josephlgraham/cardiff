Calendar

—

Council meetings, county collection days, the market, the garden dates, and the feast days people here still keep. Twelve months of it, and everything on it stays where you can find it again.

A few of these days will look unfamiliar, and they got here the way nearly everything here got here. The mines hired from home, so home came with them. Welsh and Scottish families came to Cardiff in the 1880s. Slovak and Ukrainian families came to Brookside, better than a third of the town by 1910, and built St. Nicholas so they would have somewhere to keep their own year in. Every one of those families was an immigrant, and what they carried was a calendar: days worth marking, food to cook on them, a reason to get everybody in one room. A hundred years on, those are not anybody's foreign customs. They are just what people here do in February and in August.
