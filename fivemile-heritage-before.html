Before the coal

It is easy to look at these three places now and assume the coal was always the point. For about fifty years it was not. The first settlers reached the Cardiff area in the 1830s, and the Crocker family held that land for the next fifty years with the coal sitting underneath it, unbothered and unremarked on. Brookside was farm bottomland from 1838, and it stayed farm country for close to fifty years before anybody opened a mine. Even the name is plain. There is no Brookside family behind it and there never was, only a description of a town built beside a creek. What this chapter holds is farmland with something valuable underneath it that nobody had yet decided to go and get, which is worth keeping in mind when you read what happens next.
