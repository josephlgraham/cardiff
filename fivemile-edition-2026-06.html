Monthly

June ran about as warm as usual and came in wet, and Five Mile Creek rose over the high water line twice.

This edition was put together on September 15, 2026, after the month was over, from the records as they stood. Its calls were worked out by the same rule as every other month, from what was on file before each month began.

Weather

🌙Coldest54° on Jun 4

The average of the highs and lows came to 77.6 degrees, 0.7 degrees cooler than normal for June.

Our station measured 10.37 inches of rain, against a normal of 4.78 at the Birmingham airport. The wettest day was June 20, with 4.83 inches.

At the Birmingham airport, whose record goes back to 1930, it was the seventh wettest June on the books.

Five Mile Creek at Republic
🌊Highest8.47 ft on Jun 20
🪨Lowest1.23 ft on Jun 7
📈Days at high water5

It reached the high water line, 3.5 feet at Republic, on five days, in two separate rises.

Its highest reading, 8.47 feet on June 20, was higher than the 4.22 feet the creek reaches in a typical June since 2008.

The high water line is the one the masthead uses. It is not a flood stage, because this gauge does not have one.

How the calls did

The calls for June

Temperature
🛰️NOAANo call
📜FIVEMILENo call

Set against the same month in 1991 to 2020 at the Birmingham airport, June finished in the warm third for temperature and the wet third for rain.

NOAA's outlook, issued May 31, had no lean either way on temperature, so there was nothing to score.

On rain it leaned slightly toward a wetter than normal month, and that was right too.

FIVEMILE made no call for June, because the record had no clear lean after a middle May.

Since August 2014, NOAA's temperature outlook for these three towns has been right 62 times and missed 33 times, with no lean 46 times. FIVEMILE has not made a call yet since January 2026. A call made by guessing would be right about one time in three.

NOAA's Climate Prediction Center gives these three towns a 40 percent chance of a warmer than normal July, and no lean either way on rain.

FIVEMILE's call is a warmer than normal July. After a warm June, July has come in warm in 14 of 26 years at the airport.

FIVEMILE does not call rain. In the airport record a wet month is followed by a wet one no more often than chance would have it.

The old belief

The dog days

The dog days are the hottest stretch of the summer.

The dog days run from July 3 to August 11, named for Sirius, the Dog Star, which lines up with the sun in the middle of them.

The airport record can test it. It holds when the hottest day of the summer, June through September, falls between July 3 and August 11. When the hottest reading is tied, any tied day inside the dog days counts. In the 96 years on the record, it held in 43.

Where this comes from The month's weather is the FIVEMILE weather station. Normals, ranks, records and the thirds a month is scored in are the official National Weather Service record kept at the Birmingham airport since 1930, set against 1991 to 2020. The creek is the USGS gauge on Fivemile Creek near Republic. The outlook is the monthly outlook from NOAA's Climate Prediction Center, read at the middle of the three towns. The stories are the ones the news page ran, the sightings are iNaturalist's research grade records, and the dates are the calendar's. FIVEMILE's call is a rule written down in advance: it looks at how the month just gone finished and at what followed every earlier year that started the same way. Every sentence here was written ahead of time and filled in from those records, and no edition is changed after it runs.
