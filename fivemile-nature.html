🍃The nature desk

Every month along the lower end of Five Mile Creek has a handful of things worth walking outside to look at, and most of them are gone again in three weeks.

Frogs calling in February, the dawn chorus at its loudest in April, fireflies in the wet ground in June, goldenrod and the first southbound birds in September. The creek bottoms through Graysville, Cardiff, and Brookside keep their own calendar, because water holds heat: the bottoms green up after the fence lines do and hold their color after the ridges have dropped. Season windows, frost dates, hunting season dates, and the plants and animals people around here have actually recorded on iNaturalist are all kept here.

Why the creek bottoms run late

Water holds heat longer than air or soil does, so the strip along a creek warms slower in spring and cools slower in fall than the open ground a hundred feet away. It is why the bottoms green up after the fence lines and hold their leaf color after the ridges have dropped, and why the same walk gives you a different month depending which side of the bank you take.

What people are finding

—

Anybody can file a sighting on iNaturalist and a good many people here do. These are the ones other naturalists have since agreed on, recorded along the lower end of the creek. Places are given to the town and no closer, and iNaturalist withholds the location on some records itself, most often the snakes and the salamanders.

Hunting seasons

Dates only, and that is deliberate

Season dates are listed here because they are part of the year in these three towns and because people plan around them. Locations are not, and never will be. The state sets the dates and the annual regulations are the authority on all of it. Outdoor Alabama publishes the current seasons.

The nature year

—

Where this comes from The month by month notes are written by the nature desk and kept in this site's own file. Season windows, frost dates, and the old seasonal turnings come from the same calendar data the calendar page reads, so the two can never disagree. Daylight is calculated on the page. Ground condition comes from the weather station in Cardiff. Sightings come from iNaturalist, research grade only, which means other people looked at the record and agreed on the identification. Hunting season dates are set by the Alabama Department of Conservation and Natural Resources.
