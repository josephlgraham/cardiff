021880 to 1889

The camps

Everything that follows starts here, and it starts with a sale. The Crockers sold in 1880 to Isaac Price and James Williamson, two men who wanted what was under the land rather than the land itself. Price was Welsh and Williamson was Scottish, they opened the Cardiff mine, and Price named it for Cardiff in Wales, which is how one of four places in Jefferson County came to be named for somewhere in Britain. Then they did the thing that shaped the town more than the mine did. They wrote home. Price and Williamson sent letters to friends and family in Wales offering them work, hired out of Scotland next and then France and Italy, and inside a few years Cardiff held several hundred families from across Europe. Brookside's mine opened in 1886 under Coalburg Coal and Coke and ran for thirty four years, and Sloss Iron and Steel bought it in 1887 and started burning its coal in the Birmingham furnaces. Graysville was doing none of this. It had one cotton gin, no company behind it, and a name people used for it that was not its own, which was Gin Town.

    Price and Williamson wrote to friends and family back in Wales offering them jobs. That is how Cardiff filled.

    It was Gin Town before it was Graysville

    There was one cotton gin for miles and it was here, so Gin Town is what people called it. Who the Grays were, and when the name changed, nobody seems to have written down.

    Wikipedia
