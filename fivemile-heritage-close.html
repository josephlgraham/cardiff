051920 to 1929

The mine closes

Thirty four years of work stopped inside a single year. A United Mine Workers strike and a worldwide collapse in coal prices shut the Brookside mine in 1920, and although the strike was settled in 1921 the mine never went back to work. Sloss had already moved on. It opened a by-product coke plant in North Birmingham that same year and took the Brookside ovens apart, and the photographic record the Library of Congress holds of what was left is most of what there is to look at now. Graysville lost something different, and lost it for a different reason. Alabama revoked its charter in 1927 on the grounds of inactivity, and for the next eighteen years there was no town government there at all. The one thing that arrived rather than left in this decade arrived late. Alabama Power brought electricity to Brookside in 1927, seven years after the mine that built the town had already closed.
