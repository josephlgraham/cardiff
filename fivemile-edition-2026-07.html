Monthly

July ran about as warm as usual and came in close to normal on rain, and Five Mile Creek rose over the high water line once.

This edition was put together on September 15, 2026, after the month was over, from the records as they stood. Its calls were worked out by the same rule as every other month, from what was on file before each month began.

It reached the high water line, 3.5 feet at Republic, on one day, in one separate rise.

Its highest reading, 4.69 feet on July 24, was lower than the 5.60 feet the creek reaches in a typical July since 2008.

The high water line is the one the masthead uses. It is not a flood stage, because this gauge does not have one.

The calls for July

Temperature
🛰️NOAARight
📜FIVEMILERight

Set against the same month in 1991 to 2020 at the Birmingham airport, July finished in the warm third for temperature and the wet third for rain.

NOAA's outlook, issued June 30, gave a 40 percent chance of a warmer than normal month. That was right.

FIVEMILE called it warm, because after a warm June, July had come in warm in 14 of 26 years. That was right.

Since August 2014, NOAA's temperature outlook for these three towns has been right 63 times and missed 33 times, with no lean 46 times. FIVEMILE has been right the one time it has made a call since January 2026. A call made by guessing would be right about one time in three.

NOAA's Climate Prediction Center gives these three towns a 40 percent chance of a warmer than normal August, and no lean either way on rain.

FIVEMILE's call is a warmer than normal August. After a warm July, August has come in warm in 12 of 23 years at the airport.

FIVEMILE does not call rain. In the airport record a wet month is followed by a wet one no more often than chance would have it.

Where this comes from The month's weather is the FIVEMILE weather station. Normals, ranks, records and the thirds a month is scored in are the official National Weather Service record kept at the Birmingham airport since 1930, set against 1991 to 2020. The creek is the USGS gauge on Fivemile Creek near Republic. The outlook is the monthly outlook from NOAA's Climate Prediction Center, read at the middle of the three towns. The stories are the ones the news page ran, the sightings are iNaturalist's research grade records, and the dates are the calendar's. FIVEMILE's call is a rule written down in advance: it looks at how the month just gone finished and at what followed every earlier year that started the same way. Every sentence here was written ahead of time and filled in from those records, and no edition is changed after it runs.
