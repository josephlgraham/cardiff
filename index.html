The last of Five Mile Creek

The creek rises from a spring near Chalkville and runs forty three miles west, collecting half of Jefferson County on the way. Graysville, Cardiff, and Brookside are the last towns it passes before it empties into the Locust Fork. Whatever happens upstream arrives here eventually, and for most of a century what arrived was mine tailings and coke runoff. People called it Chocolate Creek. They called it Creosote Creek. They kept their children out of it.

Then six cities formed a district in 2002, bought sixteen and a half miles of abandoned CSX rail bed, and started pulling the corridor back. Nearly sixteen miles of that is walkable now. The state tested these waters and put no restriction on eating anything caught between Brookside and Cardiff. There is an outfitter running boats from the Billingsley Trestle down to Bensko Park. There are herons working the shallows most mornings.

That recovery is the best story in this part of the county and almost nobody is telling it. This is where we tell it, alongside the ordinary useful business of three towns that share one watershed: what the water is doing, when the council meets, what is worth driving over for this weekend.

The three towns

Heritage →
Graysville

Gin Town first

It was called Gin Town before it was Graysville. The state pulled its charter in 1927, and it came back in 1945 to build its own water system.

Cardiff

Named for Wales

The men who came here to dig coal named it for Cardiff, Wales. Their sons won a state soccer cup in 1898.

Brookside

The oldest Orthodox church in Alabama

Slovak and Rusyn miners built it here in 1894, and it still serves dinner every November.
