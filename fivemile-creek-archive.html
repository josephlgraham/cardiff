The other half of it

What goes into the creek

How high the creek runs is one question about it. What goes into it is the other, and that has a page of its own: what goes into the creek sets out who holds a permit to discharge here and what the EPA says about the water.

Where this comes from The USGS gauge on Fivemile Creek near Republic, station 02457595. Every day since May 21, 1988 carries the mean depth and mean flow USGS publishes for it. The gauge's fifteen minute readings are online from October 1, 2007, and from then on each day also has its low and its high; before that the day's mean is all there is. The usual level for a time of year is the middle of every full year on file before this one, taken a week either side of the date. Seasons are whole months, the way the weather service counts them, so spring is March through May and winter runs from December into the new year. The last several months are provisional until USGS reviews them and can still move a little. The highest crest comes from the gauge's own peak record, because a flood can come and go inside a day. Republic sits upstream of all three towns, so what it reads is broadly what comes through Brookside, Cardiff, and Graysville after it.
