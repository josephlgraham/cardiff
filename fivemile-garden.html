Two dates run the whole garden year here. The last spring frost lands around March 20 and the first fall frost around November 15, and nearly every planting date in between is counted from one of them.

  • 🥔February. Potatoes and English peas, as soon as the ground is workable.
  • 🍅April. The big warm-season planting, once the frost date is behind you.
  • 🌿May. Okra and southern peas, which sit and sulk until the soil is hot.
  • 🥬August. The fall greens go in, and they cut all winter.

This is zone 7b running into 8a, on creek bottomland that drains unevenly. The month by month timing follows the Alabama Extension guide for Central Alabama, because a planting date with nothing behind it is somebody's opinion.

Why the bottoms behave differently

Cold air is heavier than warm air and runs downhill at night, so it pools in the low ground along the creek. That is why a bottomland garden can take a frost on a night the ridge above it escapes, even though the same bottoms hold moisture and warmth better through the day. Plant the tender things on the higher edge of the plot if you have the choice.
