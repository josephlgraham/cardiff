Advertising spec sheet

Three ad formats, one disclosure rule, and a list of places an ad may never appear. Written so that a reader can tell at a glance what is paid and what is not, without having to read a label, and so that the label is there anyway.

FIVEMILE · Revision 1 · August 2026 · Companion to fivemile-card-spec.html

The principle

The earlier draft of this had a .card-sponsor class, and that was a mistake worth explaining, because the reasoning behind the fix is the whole of this document.

Making an ad quiet is not the same as making it distinguishable. A muted card is still a card, and every card on this site carries the publication's credibility. Paid placement must not be allowed to borrow that, no matter how tasteful it looks. Worse, a very quiet ad is closer to native advertising, which is the format regulators and readers both dislike most, and for good reason.

So ads are not cards. Every structural property is deliberately the opposite of one.

PropertyA cardAn ad
Corners14px radiusSquare. Zero radius, sitewide, no exceptions.
Fill#FAF6EE, lighter than the page#EBE0CC, darker than the page. It sits back rather than lifting off.
Heading weight700 to 800600, capped. It can never shout like a headline.
Cardiff redAvailableNever. Red belongs to the publication.
LabelNoneAlways, above the ad, never below or beside it.

A reader who has spent a month with this site will feel the difference before they read the word. The word is there anyway, because feeling is not consent.

The label

One word, chosen once, used everywhere. My recommendation is ADVERTISEMENT.

"Sponsors" sounds friendlier and that is exactly the problem with it. It is the word a business uses when it would rather the reader not think about money changing hands, and readers know it. "Advertisement" is what a small-town paper has printed above the back page for a hundred years. It is plain, it is unambiguous, and using it signals that you are not trying to blur anything. The friendliness you lose is worth less than the trust you keep.

Use SUPPORTED BY only for the third format below, where a business is underwriting a whole section rather than buying a display slot. That is a different arrangement and deserves a different word.

PlacementAbove the ad, always. A label underneath is read after the fact and does not count.
TypeDM Mono, 10px, uppercase, .2em letter spacing, muted. Small but never smaller than this.
ScopeLabels the block, not each ad. Two ads in one labelled block is fine. An unlabelled ad anywhere is not.

The formats

01 · Notice ad

.ad-block > .ad · the default · one or two per block

The workhorse, and the one most local businesses will buy. A small mark, a name, a line about what they do, and a way to reach them. It looks like a card pinned to a board at the feed store, which is roughly what it is.

02 · Banner

.ad-block > .ad-banner · one per page maximum

For a business that wants the whole width, usually seasonal. Still square, still darker than the page, still capped at weight 600. It is bigger than a notice ad and it is not louder, which is a distinction worth holding onto when somebody asks to make theirs stand out.

03 · Supporter line

.ad-line · underwriting, not display

One quiet line at the foot of a section, for a business underwriting an ongoing feature rather than buying a slot. This is the format most likely to be abused, so the rule is strict: a supporter's name appears at the bottom of the section and nowhere else in it. They do not get a mention in the copy, a photograph, or a say in what the section covers.

Supported byThe garden page this month is supported by Adamsville Feed and Seed.
NeverA supporter never appears inside the content they support. If the feed store underwrites the garden page, the garden page does not recommend the feed store, quote the feed store, or photograph the feed store. Break this once and the section is worthless to every reader who notices.

04 · House ad

.ad.house · your own, in an empty slot

When a slot has no advertiser, it either disappears or it carries a house ad. Both are correct and disappearing is the default. Use a house ad only when you want the slot visibly for sale, and label it the same way as everything else, because it occupies an ad slot and readers should not have to work out that this one is different.

Where ads may and may not go

PageAllowedWhy
HomeOne block, below the news section. Never above the Right Now row.The reason people come is the top of the page. Selling it would be selling the only thing that works.
News, Almanac, Calendar, Guide, Heritage, GardenOne block per page.Normal inventory.
Town hallsNone. Enforced in code.This page carries council contacts and public meeting times. Paid placement next to civic information is the single fastest way to lose the standing the whole site depends on.
AboutNone. Enforced in code.The page where you say who you are is not for sale.

Hard capOne ad block per page, holding at most two ads. No page ever shows more. This is a limit on inventory, not a starting point to negotiate up from, and it is the reason the ads will be worth something.

Where money touches the writing

The rule

An advertiser buys a slot. An advertiser does not buy coverage, placement, a mention, favourable treatment, or advance notice of anything. There is no arrangement in which paying gets a business written about.

If a current advertiser becomes newsPublish it and disclose the relationship in the piece, in a plain sentence. "The canoe company advertises on this site." Do not quietly pull the ad and pretend the relationship did not exist.
If the news is unflatteringPublish it anyway, disclose it, and expect to lose the advertiser. Decide now, while it is hypothetical and cheap, that this is what will happen.
RecommendationsIf a piece recommends a business that advertises, say so in that piece. Every time, even when it is obvious.
RefusalsNo political ads, no candidates, no ballot measures, no anything you would be uncomfortable seeing next to a story. Write the refusal list before the first offer, because after an offer it stops being a principle and becomes an argument.

How it runs

Datafivemile-ads.json. Each entry: id, name, line, cta, url, logo, format, pages, starts, ends, house.
ExpiryAn ad renders only when today falls between starts and ends. Expired ads vanish without anyone doing anything. This is the whole billing system at this scale.
Empty slotsRender nothing at all, or a house ad. Never an empty bordered box.
No trackingNo pixels, no third party scripts, no impression counts, no click counts. If an advertiser asks for numbers, tell them plainly that the site does not track readers and that you can tell them how many people visit the page.
What you can honestly reportPage views from your own analytics, and how long the ad ran. Nothing else, and do not imply otherwise.
TermsA one page written note to every advertiser: what they get, which pages, how long, what happens if they are ever in a story, and that you edit copy for length and house style. Send it before the first payment. It prevents the whole category of argument that kills small publications.

Before the first ad goes live
